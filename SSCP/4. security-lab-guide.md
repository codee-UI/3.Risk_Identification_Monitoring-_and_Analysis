# Security Assessment Lab: Study Guide

Step-by-step working principles, pros/cons, deployments and a glossary. The matching interactive simulation is `security-lab.html`.

## Host scanning

Hardening a host so it offers only the services its role needs, then scanning to prove it.

### Working principle

1. **Define the host’s role**: Policy states what this host must do (e.g. web server) and nothing more.
2. **Disable unneeded services**: Remove or stop anything not critical to the role; unused software is forgotten and unpatched.
3. **Disable insecure services**: Replace legacy tools built for private LANs (Telnet, FTP, rlogin) with secure alternatives.
4. **Port-map the host**: Scanners use many techniques to list open ports and services.
5. **Compare with policy**: Any service found that policy doesn’t list is a finding to remove or justify.
6. **Patch, document, re-scan**: Secure today is not secure tomorrow: repeat after every change.

### Pros

- Smaller attack surface
- Cheap, repeatable, automatable
- Finds forgotten software

### Cons

- Only a point-in-time snapshot
- Scans can disturb fragile systems
- Needs accurate policy baseline

### Where it is deployed

| Domain | Examples |
|---|---|
| Commercial | CIS Benchmarks hardening, Nessus / Qualys / OpenVAS scans for PCI DSS |
| Defense | DISA STIG hardening and continuous scanning of military servers |
| Other | Cloud golden images, hospital and utility servers |

## IDS / IPS traffic testing

Test whether the monitoring system catches every traffic type an attacker might use.

### Working principle

1. **Single-packet patterns**: Minimum function: search one packet for an exploit signature.
2. **Multi-packet (stateful) patterns**: IDS remembers and reassembles packets, then inspects the combined payload.
3. **Obfuscated data**: Payload converted ASCII↔hex↔Unicode; IDS must normalise it back to match signatures.
4. **Fragmented data**: IP fragments and overlapping fragments (teardrop) are reassembled as the target would.
5. **Protocol-embedded attacks**: Decode DNS, SSL, HTTP, FTP, SQL like a host to find exploits inside application data.
6. **Flooding detection**: Threshold alerts, e.g. over 10 half-open connections in 2 s to one host (DoS).
7. **IPS monitoring**: IPS sits inline and acts (blocks); IDS only reports. Risk: good traffic rejected.
8. **Security gateway testing**: Proxies and gateways apply policy rules, antivirus and Java/ActiveX filters.

### Pros

- Detects known exploit patterns
- IPS stops attacks in real time
- Gives forensic logs

### Cons

- False positives and false negatives
- Evasion by fragmentation or encoding
- Inline IPS can block legitimate traffic and complicate troubleshooting

### Where it is deployed

| Domain | Examples |
|---|---|
| Commercial | Snort, Suricata, Cisco Firepower, Palo Alto next-gen firewalls at banks and data centres |
| Defense | Government gateway sensors such as CISA EINSTEIN; military network boundaries |
| Other | Universities, ISPs, industrial plants (Zeek monitoring) |

## Wireless testing

Finding rogue access points and checking that authorised ones are encrypted and authenticated.

### Working principle

1. **Understand the risk**: One unsecured AP is like a network port in the parking lot.
2. **Scan wired side**: Vulnerability scanner looks for AP-vendor MAC addresses and HTTP management pages.
3. **Walk the RF spectrum**: Handheld scanner finds SSIDs and MACs not owned by the company, across all bands (a/b/g/n).
4. **Automatic detection**: Authorised APs and clients report rogue RF; a switch port can be shut automatically.
5. **Lock down the switch**: MAC filtering ties each port to a known MAC so unapproved devices can’t join.
6. **Test authorised APs**: Require encryption, 802.1X authentication, SSID naming, and L2/L3/L4 filters.
7. **Know attacker methods**: War driving, war dialing (old modems, ICS/SCADA) and war flying (drones).

### Pros

- Reveals hidden entry points
- Tools are cheap (Kismet, NetStumbler)
- Supports automatic port shutdown

### Cons

- Periodic walks leave gaps between scans
- Must cover every band and standard
- Legacy WEP/WPA-PSK can be cracked (Aircrack-ng)

### Where it is deployed

| Domain | Examples |
|---|---|
| Commercial | Cisco, Aruba and Mist wireless intrusion prevention in retail, offices, hospitals |
| Defense | Wireless-free zones and drone/RF sweeps at secure facilities |
| Other | Airports, campuses, PCI DSS wireless scans |

## Pen-test modes & ethics

Ethical penetration testing is contractual: signed RoE, authorisation and confidentiality.

### Working principle

1. **Rules of engagement**: Signed contract defines scope, methods, liability; without it a tester risks criminal and civil liability.
2. **Zero knowledge (opaque box)**: Outsider with only public information: most realistic, most expensive.
3. **Partial knowledge**: Client shares IPs, network layouts, SSIDs: the most common test.
4. **Full knowledge (transparent box)**: Internal team knows everything; finds weaknesses before attackers do.
5. **Blind test**: IT staff don’t know; measures detection and response.
6. **Double-blind test**: Neither IT nor security knows; tests the incident management program.
7. **Red teaming & bug bounty**: Aggressive, TTP-based simulation; bounty hunters stay ethical by following program terms and disclosing only to the owner.

### Pros

- Proves whether controls work in practice
- Exercises detection and response
- Compliance frameworks recognise it

### Cons

- Zero-knowledge tests are costly and need skilled testers
- Can disrupt operations
- Scope limits may miss other problems

### Where it is deployed

| Domain | Examples |
|---|---|
| Commercial | Banks under TIBER-EU / CBEST red-team schemes; HackerOne and Bugcrowd bounties |
| Defense | Military and government red teams (e.g. DoD Red Teams) |
| Other | Healthcare, SaaS, automotive and IoT product testing |

## Five phases of pen testing

A rigorous methodology that directly affects risk assessment of the test itself.

### Working principle

1. **1. Chartering / scoping**: RoE drafted: scope, methods, constraints, authorisers, incident reporting, legal limits.
2. **2. Discovery**: Reconnaissance maps systems in scope (skipped if RoE names exact targets).
3. **3. Scanning**: Find weaknesses and fingerprint systems so exploits can be crafted.
4. **4. Exploitation**: Deliver exploit, document the compromise, and withdraw without leaving a new hole.
5. **5. Reporting**: Executive summary and recommendations; return all materials, including captured credentials.

### Pros

- Repeatable, auditable process
- Limits damage through the RoE
- Produces actionable recommendations

### Cons

- No single standard (OSSTMM, NIST SP 800-115, PTES differ)
- Methodology must be documented in the RoE
- Exploitation always carries real risk

### Where it is deployed

| Domain | Examples |
|---|---|
| Commercial | Metasploit, Burp Suite, Nmap used by consultancies for ISO 27001 / PCI DSS pen tests |
| Defense | Accreditation testing of weapon-system and mission networks |
| Other | Critical infrastructure, e-government portals |

## SOC reports

AICPA attestation reports (SSAE 18/20) about a service organisation’s controls.

### Working principle

1. **SOC 1**: Controls over financial reporting (ICFR).
2. **Type I vs Type II**: Type I: design at a point in time. Type II: operating effectiveness over a period (6+ months, usually annual).
3. **SOC 2**: Trust Services Criteria: Security, Availability, Confidentiality, Processing Integrity, Privacy.
4. **SOC 3**: Public, less technical summary of a SOC 2.
5. **SOC for Cybersecurity**: Evaluates the cybersecurity risk-management program using the criteria.

### Pros

- Builds customer trust
- One report answers many vendor questionnaires
- Type II shows ongoing due diligence

### Cons

- Costly and time-consuming
- Reports are point-in-time or period-limited
- SOC 1/2 are restricted-use documents

### Where it is deployed

| Domain | Examples |
|---|---|
| Commercial | AWS, Microsoft Azure, Salesforce and payroll providers publish SOC 1/2/3 |
| Defense | Contractors supporting government outsourcing show SOC 2 alongside FedRAMP |
| Other | Hospitals’ cloud vendors, fintech and SaaS start-ups |

## Compliance & communicating findings

Compliance is built into every life-cycle step; audit results are reported to leadership.

### Working principle

1. **Adopt a standard**: Compliance starts by accepting a standard for how the business operates.
2. **Embed in life cycle**: Applied to development, use, maintenance, disposal, training and evaluation.
3. **Audit draft**: Draft report to management: scope, objective, findings, recommendations.
4. **Management response**: Fix errors, plan and schedule actions, or record disagreement.
5. **Record fixed issues**: Even issues fixed before the final report are logged so auditors can confirm they stay fixed.
6. **Final report**: Executive summary for senior managers and the board audit committee; detailed report for those fixing findings.
7. **Feed back to monitoring**: Leaders may ask for more frequent or detailed monitoring.

### Pros

- Meets laws and regulator expectations
- Keeps leadership informed of risk
- Creates accountability

### Cons

- Adds cost
- Risk of checkbox compliance
- Findings can go stale if not followed up

### Where it is deployed

| Domain | Examples |
|---|---|
| Commercial | SOX, PCI DSS and ISO 27001 audits |
| Defense | FISMA / RMF authorisation and continuous monitoring |
| Other | HIPAA in healthcare, GDPR in privacy programs |
