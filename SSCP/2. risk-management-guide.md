# Risk Management: Companion Guide

Mirrors the Risk Lab simulation: concept, working principle, pros/cons, real-world use.

## 1. Frameworks
**NIST RMF (SP 800-37 Rev. 2)**, 7 steps: Prepare → Categorize → Select → Implement → Assess → Authorize → Monitor (loop).
**SP 800-39** frames the process as Frame → Assess → Respond → Monitor. **SP 800-53 Rev. 5** is the control catalog; **SP 800-171** protects CUI in contractor systems.
**NIST CSF:** Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover (voluntary, "a la carte"). **ISO/IEC 27000:** audit framework for an ISMS; 27005 covers risk.

| Pros | Cons |
|---|---|
| Repeatable, free (NIST), aligns with ISO 27005/31000 | Documentation heavy; can become checkbox work |

**Used in:** US federal agencies and FedRAMP (government), CMMC/800-171 contractors (defense), CSF in banks and healthcare, ISO 27001 certification in global commercial firms.

## 2. Anatomy of a risk event (SP 800-30)
Threat source → *initiates* → threat event → *exploits* → vulnerability (+ predisposing conditions, security controls) → *causing* → adverse impact → organizational risk.
Assessment steps: prepare; identify threat sources/events; vulnerabilities and predisposing conditions; likelihood; magnitude of impact; determine risk; communicate results; maintain assessment.
Threat events are not accidents: they originate with humans pursuing goals.
**Pros:** shows where controls break the chain. **Cons:** likelihood is estimated; little data for new tech.
**Used in:** ad hoc assessments after new ransomware, mission risk reviews for defense systems, SCADA reviews in energy.

## 3. Quantitative assessment
- **SLE = AV × EF** (e.g. $36,000 × 25% = $9,000)
- **ARO** = events per year (once in 10 years = 0.1)
- **ALE = SLE × ARO** ($9,000 × 0.1 = $900)

Steps: value asset → estimate EF → SLE → ARO → ALE → compare with control cost.
**Pros:** money language, supports ROI and cost-benefit. **Cons:** guesses create false precision; intangibles are hard to value.
**Used in:** cyber-insurance pricing, bank operational risk, justifying firewall/backup spend, defense logistics planning.

## 4. Qualitative assessment
Likelihood × impact on a Very Low to Very High scale, built from scenarios and workshops.

| Likelihood \ Impact | VL | L | M | H | VH |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Very High / High | VL | L | M | H | VH |
| Moderate | VL | L | M | M | H |
| Low | VL | L | L | L | M |
| Very Low | VL | VL | VL | L | L |

**Pros:** fast, no financial data needed. **Cons:** subjective; hard to justify spend from a "Level 5" label.
**Used in:** agency workshops, healthcare safety reviews, defense impact categorization, startup risk registers.

## 5. Risk treatment
Options: **Accept**, **Avoid**, **Mitigate** (safeguards prevent, countermeasures react), **Share/Transfer** (insurance, partners; responsibility stays with you).
Flow: total risk → treatment → residual risk → acceptable? No: iterate. Yes: update risk register.
Formula: **Total risk − control effectiveness = residual risk**.
Example: ransomware ALE $250,000 → cloud rehosting $150,000 → MSSP with performance bond $50,000 → accepted residual.
Insurance caveats: slow claims, cancellable at short notice, no substitute for controls, compliance may limit reliance.
Action plan: owner, resources, dates, milestones, reporting. Include ongoing costs (training, licensing, support) in cost-benefit; do not spend $10,000 to protect a $5,000 asset.
**Used in:** cloud plus MSSP (commercial), redundancy (aviation/defense), insurance after baseline controls (retail), market exit (finance).

## 6. Threat modeling
- **STRIDE:** Spoofing, Tampering, Repudiation, Information disclosure, Denial of service, Elevation of privilege.
- **DREAD:** Damage, Reproducibility, Exploitability, Affected users, Discoverability (score 1-10, average).
- **Attack trees:** goal broken into sub-goals. **PASTA:** staged, risk-centric analysis.
Also considers non-repudiation, authentication, privacy and safety beyond CIA; results go in the RAR.
**Pros:** finds design flaws early. **Cons:** needs skill; DREAD is subjective.
**Used in:** Microsoft SDL (software), attack-tree analysis of defense links, automotive/medical device safety, mobile and web apps.

## 7. Legal and regulatory compliance
Laws are a moral minimum; policy may add but never conflict. Cite laws in governance and training. Privacy data (PII, PHI, PI) is both asset and liability. Track changing, conflicting multi-jurisdiction rules.
**Used in:** HIPAA (health), GDPR and PCI DSS (commercial), FISMA/800-171 (government and defense contractors).
