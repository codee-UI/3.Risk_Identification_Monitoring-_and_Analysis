1. Frameworks: NIST RMF, CSF, ISO 27000

The NIST RMF (SP 800-37 Rev. 2) is a 7-step repeatable loop. Press play, or click a step.

SP 800-39 view: Frame, Assess, Respond, Monitor

NIST CSF

Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover. Voluntary, “a la carte”, simpler for non-federal organizations.

ISO/IEC 27000 series

Audit framework for an Information Security Management System (ISMS). Widely adopted outside the US; 27005 covers risk.

SP 800-53 / 800-171

800-53 is the control catalog. 800-171 protects Controlled Unclassified Information held by private suppliers.

2. Anatomy of a risk event

Per NIST SP 800-30: a threat source initiates a threat event that exploits a vulnerability, causing an adverse impact. Controls can break the chain.

Assessment steps (SP 800-30): prepare, then identify threat sources and events, vulnerabilities and predisposing conditions, likelihood, magnitude of impact, determine risk; then communicate results and maintain the assessment.

3. Quantitative assessment: SLE, ARO, ALE

SLE = AV × EF. ALE = SLE × ARO. Defaults are the wiring-center fire example from the text.

Max sensible yearly control cost

Rule of thumb: do not spend more than the loss you prevent. A control costing more per year than the ALE is hard to justify.

5. Risk treatment and residual risk

Total risk minus control effectiveness is residual risk. Watch the text’s ransomware example fall from $250,000 to a $50,000 accepted residual.

Accept

Take no action; cost of control exceeds loss.

Avoid

Stop the activity, e.g. close an unsafe branch.

Mitigate

Safeguards prevent; countermeasures react.

Share/Transfer

Insurance or partners. Accountability stays with you.
