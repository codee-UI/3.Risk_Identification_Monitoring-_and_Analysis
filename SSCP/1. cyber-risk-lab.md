# Cyber Risk Lab: Study Guide

Text companion to the interactive web page. Same topics, same order, with the calculators written as formulas you can try by hand.

## 1. Cyber Kill Chain (7 stages)

| # | Stage | Working principle | Defender response | ATT&CK tactic |
|---|-------|-------------------|-------------------|---------------|
| 1 | Reconnaissance | Research the target | Limit public data, monitor scans | Reconnaissance |
| 2 | Weaponization | Pair exploit with payload | Threat intel, file controls | Resource Development |
| 3 | Delivery | Send the weapon (phishing, USB) | Email filtering, training | Initial Access |
| 4 | Exploitation | Flaw is triggered, code runs | Patching, hardening | Execution |
| 5 | Installation | Persistent backdoor | Endpoint detection, least privilege | Persistence, Privilege Escalation, Defense Evasion |
| 6 | Command and Control | Host calls home | DNS/egress filtering, IOC blocking | Command and Control |
| 7 | Actions on Objectives | Theft, ransom, sabotage | Segmentation, DLP, backups | Collection, Exfiltration, Impact |

**Simulation by hand:** place a control at stage N. The attack succeeds only through stages before N, so the earlier the block, the less damage.

- **Pros:** simple, good for layered defense planning.
- **Cons:** assumes a linear attack; weak for insiders and cloud abuse.
- **Used in:** defense (Lockheed Martin origin, government SOCs), commercial SOCs at banks and retailers, training exercises.

## 2. MITRE ATT&CK

14 tactics (the *why*), each with techniques, sub-techniques and detections (the *how*): Reconnaissance, Resource Development, Initial Access, Execution, Persistence, Privilege Escalation, Defense Evasion, Credential Access, Discovery, Lateral Movement, Collection, Command and Control, Exfiltration, Impact. Example: T1547 Boot or Logon Autostart Execution (Persistence).

- **Pros:** shared vocabulary built from observed attacks; includes detections and mitigations.
- **Cons:** very large; describes behavior, not likelihood.
- **Used in:** vendor detections tagged by technique, red team planning, government threat modeling, sector comparisons against known threat groups.

## 3. Risk management terms

- **Threat:** event with potential to harm operations, assets or people. APTs are the most damaging.
- **Vulnerability:** weakness, the "hole in the fence". Controls must be maintained and monitored to work.
- **Impact:** magnitude of harm. Existential risk means total loss of the organization.
- **Likelihood:** annualized probability. Monthly = 12.0, every 10 years = 0.10.
- **Threat agents:** intentional (insiders, hackers, fraudsters, competitors, APTs), unintentional (errors, misconfigurations), hazards (storms, power failure).

**Calculator:** `Expected yearly loss = likelihood x impact x (1 - control strength)`
Example: 12 per year x $50k x (1 - 0.4) = $360k per year.

**Nines:** 99% fails 1 in 100; 99.9% 1 in 1,000; 99.99% 1 in 10,000; 99.999% 1 in 100,000.

**Risk register columns:** Ref, risk (what and how), consequences, likelihood, adequacy of existing controls, consequence rating, likelihood rating, level of risk, priority. Fed by audits, incident reports, penetration tests, vulnerability assessments and tickets. Partition by business unit with care so global risks stay visible.

- **Pros:** business-language decisions, one central view.
- **Cons:** subjective likelihood, hard for new technology.
- **Used in:** insurance and underwriting, board risk reviews, defense and government assessments, hospital and airline critical process planning.

## 4. Threat modeling

**STRIDE**

| Letter | Threat | Property violated |
|--------|--------|-------------------|
| S | Spoofing | Authentication |
| T | Tampering | Integrity |
| R | Repudiation | Non-repudiation |
| I | Information disclosure | Confidentiality |
| D | Denial of Service | Availability |
| E | Elevation of privilege | Authorization |

**DREAD score** = average of Damage, Reproducibility, Exploitability, Affected users, Discoverability (each 1 to 10). 7 or more: high; 4 to 6.9: medium; below 4: low (a common convention).

**PASTA:** staged, risk-centric process: define objectives, identify threats, analyze vulnerabilities, simulate attacks. **Attack trees:** goal at the root, sub-goals as branches.

Results go into a Risk Assessment Report (RAR).

- **Pros:** finds design flaws early; covers privacy and safety.
- **Cons:** needs skill and time; DREAD is subjective.
- **Used in:** software design reviews (STRIDE came from Microsoft), defense system analysis, medical and automotive safety.

## 5. Risk visibility

- **IOC types:** URLs, domain names, IP addresses, hash values. Found via logs, partners, vendors, forums and government sources.
- **IOA:** an IOC signaled by ongoing events. **Precursor:** hints at future activity, used to set a "risk throttle".
- **Sharing:** IOCs, analyst notes and remediation steps through ISACs and ISAOs. Hesitation exists because of exposure, but sharing speeds remediation.
- **CVSS** (FIRST.org, created 2005, v4 in 2023):

| Score | Severity |
|-------|----------|
| 0 | None |
| 0.1 to 3.9 | Low |
| 4.0 to 6.9 | Medium |
| 7.0 to 8.9 | High |
| 9.0 to 10.0 | Critical |

- **Pros:** faster response, common patch-priority scale.
- **Cons:** CVSS ignores your asset context; some IOCs are slow to detect.
- **Used in:** financial, health and energy ISACs, vulnerability scanners, national vulnerability databases.
